767.nest_r

The NEural Simulation Tool (NEST) is a simulator for spiking neural network models that focuses on the dynamics, size, and structure of neural systems rather than on the exact morphology of individual neurons. It is used in computational neuroscience to model and study behavior of large networks of neurons and in neuromorphic computing as a reference for correctness and performance. The models describe single neuron and synapse behavior and their connections. Different mechanisms of plasticity can be used to investigate artificial learning and help shed light on the fundamental principles of how the brain works.

Official description is available at https://www.spec.org/cpu2026/Docs/benchmarks/767.nest_r/767.nest_r.html.

Please note that the -Ofast compilation also additionally used option -fno-finite-math-only.

DISCLAIMER: All the SPEC results are "non-reportable". The measurements were taken in order to compare different compilers and compiler options, not different hardware, please do not attempt to make any comparisons of the different machines used. Moreover, we have broken various SPEC benchmarking rules, we have even patched some benchmark sources to be able to compile them with -fstrict-aliasing, build with -fno-common, and so forth. And the data may be just wrong, the scripts generating all the fancy graphs and tables have been known to contain bugs in the past. On the other hand, this is the data that we use to evaluate where GCC stands and we believe that overall this comparison of compilers is a useful one.
